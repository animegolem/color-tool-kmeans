#+TITLE: Color Tool 2.0
#+OPTIONS: num:t

* Objective Function
A gift between artists. An instrument for seeing the colour and value structure of a picture or a film frame:
open something, and its palette and its lights and darks are just there, quick enough to play with. Scrub a film
and watch the palette breathe with it. Nothing waits on a server and nothing leaves your machine.

It started as a present to a friend who paints, and it should still feel like one: calm, honest about what it
is doing, and never in the way of looking.

* Problem and Outcome
** Problem
Color Tool 1.x has not worked properly since the refactor that began in September 2026:
- It is slow in ways the math does not explain. On 2026-11-19 a palette that takes 0.2 s on its own arrived
  3.9 s and then 17 s after it was asked for, because nothing cancels superseded work or limits how much runs at
  once (see [[*What We've Proven]]).
- A review of its implicit state machines found 36 ordering bugs across 9 models. They come from a few habits:
  state kept in two places, asynchronous results with no owner, and no cancellation or bounds.
- It does not look like the September 2026 design, which was never built.

** Outcome
A 2.0 that runs as a website and as the same app on the desktop. One job runner owns all the work, and the app's
behaviour is a small set of state machines written down in TLA+ before they are written in code. Colors and
Values come first, then Batch and Exports.

* Explicit Non-Goals
- Repairing 1.x forward. 2.0 rebuilds the app layer (ADR-0001).
- A system-webview shell: Tauri, or a webview mode of any other framework (ADR-0002).
- Computing on a server, or uploading anything the user opens.
- A third-party state-machine library. The machines are our own code.
- Batch and Exports in the first cut. They are the second cut, not dropped.

* Domain Model Glossary
** Studies
- Source :: The thing being studied: a still image, or one frame of a video at a given time.
  /Avoid/: file, media, input
- Job :: One piece of work the app does for a Source, such as a palette, a value study, a frame grab or a strip.
  /Avoid/: task, request, run
- Job Runner :: The one owner of every Job: it decides what runs, in what order, how many at once, and which
  results are still wanted.
  /Avoid/: scheduler, queue, worker pool

* UI Goals and Aesthetic Concept
The September 2026 study design is the reference: =RAG/design-system/2026-09/= and the design bundles under
=RAG/=. It is redrawn in Paper and reviewed in rounds with Golem before the state machines are written, because
the screens decide which UI states the machines must model.

Rules found so far:
- In a 3D view, a label never covers the solid, at any angle. Each frame, project the solid's vertices, take
  their convex hull, and place each label just outside it along the label's own direction; fade a label whose
  direction points at or away from the viewer. Prototype: =.scratch/prototype/volume.html= (2026-11-21, zero
  overlaps over 2,100 angles).

* Verification Policy
** Verification and Acceptance
- A check never seen to fail counts for nothing. Every test and every TLA+ rule fails on a planted fault before
  it passes.
- The 1.x state-machine gate's 36 findings are 2.0's checklist. Each finding's rule must hold in 2.0, as a check
  that was first seen to fail.
- Results are stated in the house tiers: proved, classically verified, tested, sampled, asserted.

* Architectural Decision Record
** ADR-0001 2.0 rebuilds the app layer instead of repairing 1.x
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: adr-0001
:STATUS: accepted
:END:
Golem, 2026-11-19: the app "has never really worked post the start of this refactor". The September correctness
branch, 22 careful commits, still left 17 of the 36 findings reproducing, including every high-severity one the
gate could reach. So 2.0 keeps what is measured to be sound: =color-core= (the math), the export fixtures, and
the state-machine gate. It rebuilds the state, the views and the shell. The 1.x app stays in the repo as a
reference until 2.0 replaces it, and =RAG/PROJECT-RECORD.md= is frozen as history.

** ADR-0002 Electron and Svelte 5, not a system webview or a GPU-drawn UI
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: adr-0002
:STATUS: accepted
:END:
The website and the desktop app are one Svelte 5 codebase. The desktop app runs it in Electron's own Chromium,
so the engine is the same on every machine. =color-core= runs as WebAssembly in both. Golem ruled out system
webviews (Tauri, MyGo's web mode) as the worst of both: a browser engine we do not control. The cost is size
(installers of about 100 to 150 MB) and memory.

Considered: GPUIX (React drawn on the GPU, with Rust linked natively) and MyGo native UI (Go drawn on the GPU,
with a WebAssembly browser build). Both give small native apps, but both make the website a canvas-drawn build
that is newer and riskier than an ordinary page, and MyGo cannot link the Rust math directly. Electron does
everything 2.0 needs without either risk.

** ADR-0003 One Job Runner owns all work
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: adr-0003
:STATUS: proposed
:END:
Pages never start work. They send events to the app's state machines, and the machines ask the Job Runner for
Jobs. The Runner runs on-screen work first, cancels superseded Jobs, bounds how many run at once, and delivers
a result only where it is still wanted. This is the fix for the pile-up measured on 2026-11-19. It stays
proposed until the first proof shows it.

* What We've Proven
** 2026-11-19 The palette math is not what made 1.x slow
- Tried: =color_core::analyze= on the 1280x720 frame from that morning's session, with the logged parameters
  (82 clusters, quality 2, 40 iterations at most, seed 1), on an M-series Mac under heavy load (load average 16
  to 32 on 16 cores).
- Showed: 0.16 to 0.2 s per palette, always reaching the 40-iteration cap. 16 clusters took 0.04 s and settled
  in 10 iterations.
- In the app that morning, the same work arrived 3.9 s and 17.4 s after it was asked for, while a barcode strip of
  3,608 frames ran for 27 s alongside it. So most of the wait was contention and queueing.
- Rerun: owed. The bench was a throwaway program; see Live Work.
** 2026-11-21 Live regrade fits in a frame, and e-means beats Lloyd per pass
- Tried: the regrade spike (=spike/regrade/=, PR #7): a WebGL2 regrade by cluster, with streaming e-means
  re-clustering a 1080p film every frame; and a JS port of e-means (faiss, MIT) against Lloyd on two paintings.
- Showed: a 4.6 MP still regrades in 1.3 to 3.4 ms; 1080p at 60 frames a second with per-frame re-clustering
  missed 6 of 1,145 frames. At equal passes e-means' error is far below Lloyd's; five epochs match 40 Lloyd
  iterations in about a sixth of the time. color-core's own Lloyd reaches its 40-iteration cap at 25,000 samples.
- Not shown: Electron, hand feel, HDR. Rerun: =spike/regrade/RESULTS.md=.
